Release Notes

What changed in each version of Zigzag. Updates install by themselves, so you're always on the newest.

0.9.5

November 25, 2026

Export Your Data so you can always take everything with you, live folders, nesting tabs by dragging, Traditional Chinese, full screen that stays in the browser, an Autohide address bar, the sidebar from the screen edge, smoother scrolling with less of Zigzag's own work on every page, pages at your display's full 120 Hz, touch that feels native on a touch display, Dia's pinned tabs on import, press and hold Back or Forward for history, sign-ins and YouTube's theater mode kept after a relaunch, and password manager extensions in Settings.

New

  • Importing from Dia brings its sidebar too, as from Arc. Each Dia profile's pinned tabs are pinned in the space its bookmarks go in, split views stay side by side, Dia's favorites become favorites, and with Open Tabs on, the tabs open there come over and wait to load until clicked. Importing again adds only what's new. #511
  • Press and hold Back or Forward to see the pages that way and pick one, as in Safari and Chrome. Slide onto a page and let go to go there, or let go and click one. It works in the sidebar, the bar above the page, split view, Zigzag Mini and web apps, and a finger held on a touch display opens the same list. A right-click still opens it too, now in Zigzag's own menu. #509
  • With Settings › Tabs › Full screen videos fill set to The Whole Screen, a video's full screen now stays in the browser window. The window goes into its own full-screen space, as with View › Enter Full Screen, and the sidebar and bar step aside; move the pointer to the top to bring the bar down. In split view the video fills its own pane instead. Esc puts the window back as it was, and a window that was already in full screen stays there. The button in the video's top corner still gives it a full screen of its own, as before. Web apps, Zigzag Mini and the mini player keep the full screen they had. Thanks to Lakeland97. #462
  • Zigzag now speaks Traditional Chinese. Choose it in Settings › General › Language and restart Zigzag. Thanks to aliceric27. #505
  • Sidebar from the screen edge, in Settings › Appearance, brings the sidebar out over whatever app you're in, the way a hidden Dock comes out. Push the pointer against the edge of the screen on the sidebar's side and rest a moment: your spaces, favorites, tabs and what's playing show over that app. Click a tab, a favorite or New Tab and Zigzag comes forward on it, as from the Dock; move the pointer away and the sidebar goes back with the other app still in front. It never comes out in a corner, on the side the Dock or Stage Manager uses, or at an edge that leads to another display. Off until you turn it on. Thanks to Lakeland97. #363
  • AI apps can work in the background, with nothing on screen: turn on Settings › Automation › Run AI apps in the background (off until you do, and only with Let AI apps use Zigzag on). They get a hidden window of their own on a profile of their own, AI Apps, and can't see your windows or tabs; its space stays out of your sidebar and space lists, and you can delete the profile in Settings › Profiles. With both switches on, opening Zigzag with `--headless` runs it for them alone, with no Dock icon or window until you open it, and it quits on its own once they're done. That profile is given nothing of yours: no saved passwords, sign-ins, certificates or passkeys, none of the permissions you gave sites, none of your download rules or site routes, no reaching apps on this Mac, and its tabs never go to your Archive. Their pages start muted, can't go full screen over your screen or write to your clipboard, and their downloads go to a folder of their own, out of your downloads list and the Dock. No one is there to answer a page, so its dialogs are dismissed and the app is told. An item in the menu bar shows they're working, with Show AI Apps Window to watch them and Stop AI Apps. Tabs an AI app is using are no longer put to sleep, archived or cleared away under it. Thanks to vandenbulckjean. #214
  • Extensions that save pages (`chrome.pageCapture`) work: they can save a tab as one MHTML file, as in Chrome, with its styles, pictures and frames, which Chrome and other browsers open as the page. It's only for web pages on sites the extension can access. Of what a page loaded, only its pictures, stylesheets and fonts go in, as in Chrome, and a frame from a site the extension can't access is left out. Thanks to OnurrUgur. #488
  • File › Export Your Data… saves your spaces, pinned tabs and favorites as a bookmarks file Chrome, Safari, Firefox and other browsers import, each space a folder with its folders and pages in the sidebar's order. It can also save a Zigzag backup with your spaces, folders, split views, favorites, profiles, site settings and settings, and if you like your open tabs and history, which Zigzag restores on this Mac or another when you double-click it or choose it in the Import window, asking first and never making copies of what's already there. Passwords stay in the Passwords window, which exports them on its own. It's in Settings › General and the command bar too. #518
  • The address bar above the page can Autohide. Settings › Appearance › Address bar above the page is now Off, Autohide or Always, and if you had it on, it stays Always. With Autohide the bar goes up with the page as you scroll down, the page taking its room, and comes back when you scroll up in a new swipe, scroll to the very top, move the pointer to the window's top, press ⌘L, or tap where it was on a touch display. It stays put while you type in a page or while the page scrolls by itself, and the window buttons go and come back with it. In split view each page's bar follows its own scroll. Thanks to Lakeland97. #359
  • Live folders, as in Arc. Choose New Live Folder… from the sidebar's +, a space's menu or a folder's menu, and paste a feed's address or just a site's, or pick a GitHub list (your open pull requests, issues assigned to you, or review requests) and type your username. The folder fills itself with the newest pages and checks for more every half hour in the background, and new ones have a dot until you open them. Its menu has Mark All as Read, Refresh Now and Edit…, and says why when a feed couldn't be fetched. Drag a page out to pin it where you drop it. GitHub lists show public repositories only, since Zigzag doesn't sign in to GitHub for them.
  • Settings' long lists fold. In Privacy › Site Permissions each site is a box with its name, how many choices it has and Forget, its choices inside; in Extensions each extension is a box with its switch, and its site access and private window settings inside; in Stored by Websites each site is a box that opens to its cookies and its cache, each cleared on its own after asking, with the trash still removing everything. With more than three, boxes start folded; click one, or press Space or →, to open it, and it stays as you left it while Settings is open. With eight or more sites or extensions, a search beside the heading narrows the list, and Find a setting opens the box a match is in. Thanks to Lakeland97 for the idea. #368
  • Drag a tab to the right to nest it under the tab above, even one with no tabs under it yet, and to the left to take it back out. The line shows how far in it will land. From the keyboard, View › Nest Tab (⌃⇧→) puts the tab you're on under the tab above it, and Unnest Tab (⌃⇧←) moves it back out. Thanks to bkonia for the idea. #523
  • Live folders can follow the open issues or pull requests in the repositories a GitHub user owns, whoever opened them, so a maintainer's folder fills with what comes in. The other GitHub lists now say what they hold: Pull Requests Opened, Issues Assigned and Review Requests.
  • A live folder shows its site's icon in the sidebar, GitHub's for a GitHub list, with a small live mark on the corner so it still reads as live.
  • Hold your mouse's Back or Forward button to see the pages that way at the pointer, as with the on-screen arrows. Slide onto a page and let go to go there, or let go and click one. A quick press still goes one step. Thanks to Lakeland97 for the idea. #515
  • Turn on Settings › Zigzag Mini › Open ⇧-clicked links in the page and a ⇧-clicked link opens in Zigzag Mini right over the page you clicked from, the page dimmed behind it, instead of in a window of its own. Esc, a click on the dimmed page, ⌘W or switching tabs puts it away, and Open in Window gives the same page a Mini window of its own without reloading it. Off until you turn it on, so ⇧-click works as before. Thanks to Lakeland97. #481 #501

Fixed

  • An English page no longer offers to translate from Hindi or Marathi because of a list of language names on it, and a few short lines like place names no longer make a page read as another language. Pages in any language in your list in System Settings › Language & Region aren't offered for translation, unless you set that language to Always Translate. #510
  • On a touch display, two fingers swiped sideways on a page go back or forward again, with the same arrow, distance and flick as on the trackpad. Before, the page scrolled instead. Up and down still scrolls, a pinch still zooms, and a page that pans sideways itself under your finger, like a map or a carousel, keeps the swipe.
  • YouTube's theater mode and sign-ins that use session cookies stay after a relaunch when Zigzag reopens your last session, as in Chrome. With On launch set to Open a new window they're dropped as before, and private windows keep none.
  • On a touch display, tapping one tab right after another selects each one. Before, a quick tap on the tab next to the one you just tapped renamed it, as two taps on the same tab do.
  • On a touch display, a tap on a tab, a favorite or a sidebar button always does what a click does, even when macOS sends no click for it, and quick taps one after another each count.
  • On a touch display, dragging tabs, pinned tabs, folders, favorites and space dots works like on an iPad. Hold for about a third of a second and the item lifts under your finger right away; move a few points and it drags, following your finger. Its menu only opens if you keep still a moment longer, so it no longer pops up in the way of a drag. Once lifted, the list doesn't scroll out from under the drag, and holding a tab near the top or bottom of the list scrolls it, faster nearer the edge.
  • Settings › Passwords › Password manager lists each password manager extension you have on, like Bitwarden or 1Password, after Zigzag and iCloud Passwords. While one handles your passwords it shows checked instead of Zigzag. Choosing it turns off Zigzag's own Offer to save passwords and Fill saved passwords, and choosing Zigzag turns them back on.
  • On a touch display, a two-finger swipe goes back or forward after about 2 cm of finger travel, as it feels on the trackpad, instead of twice that, and a page that fits the window no longer slides sideways under your fingers.
  • Pages draw at the display's full refresh rate, up to 120 times a second on a ProMotion display, as in Chrome, so scrolling and animations are smoother. A page too heavy to keep up while you scroll, like signed-in GitHub, steadies at 60 until you go to another site, instead of stuttering. Settings › Developer › Feature Flags › Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps still holds every page at 60. #394
  • A tab woken from sleep keeps its page's session storage, where some sites keep you signed in. Putting the tab to sleep used to drop it, so such a site could ask you to sign in again, two-factor and all, each time you came back to the tab. It's kept only in memory while the tab sleeps, and only the page that loads first on waking gets it back. #502
  • On a touch display, a two-finger swipe sideways on a page goes back or forward even when one finger lands a moment before the other or the swipe starts a little slanted. Before, it usually scrolled the page instead.
  • Show All Tabs (⇧⌘\) opens and closes smoothly. The page starts shrinking into its card sooner, and the cards no longer hitch as they settle in, because less work runs as it opens, the overview stays ready between opens, and pages you left are made ready for it ahead of time.
  • A two-finger swipe on the trackpad shows its arrow, and the page starts to move, as soon as your fingers start sideways, instead of a moment later, so you can tell right away that it will go back or forward. A page that scrolls sideways under the pointer, like a carousel or a map, still keeps the swipe, and scrolling up and down never shows the arrow. #508
  • The menu of saved logins under a sign-in field stays under the field when you zoom the page, resize the window or a split pane, or the page moves the field, and opens in the right place on a pinched-in page. Before, it stayed where it opened, over the field, and a click meant for the field could fill a login. #378
  • Importing from Dia makes a space for every Dia profile, named as in Dia, even one with no bookmarks or pinned tabs, and Dia's sidebar tabs land in the profile you last used there when Dia doesn't say which they belong to. #511
  • Scrolling long pages that keep changing, like X, GitHub and forums such as linux.do, is smoother, because Zigzag does less of its own work meanwhile. Cookie pop-up checks wait until you stop scrolling, a forum that changes its address for each post you scroll past no longer redraws the sidebar's address each time, and a page's language is worked out away from what draws the window. Help › Report a Problem also lists any moment Zigzag paused for more than a twentieth of a second, with what it was doing then. Pages also do less of Zigzag's work as you scroll: they listen for a back or forward swipe only while one may be starting, and look for new sign-in fields only after loading or something you do.
  • A site that was too heavy for 120 Hz starts at 60 on its first page after a relaunch, instead of stuttering until Zigzag notices again; a site is remembered for a month, and clearing History or the site's data forgets it. A page that gets held near 60 changes rate once you stop scrolling, never mid-scroll. In Low Power Mode or when the Mac is hot, pages start near 60. #519
  • A feature flag you changed in Settings › Developer has a Reset button beside its switch, in place of a plain “Changed”, and a flag Zigzag starts other than WebKit, like Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps, says so under its name.
  • Letting go of a swipe that goes back or forward no longer springs the page back before it leaves. The page slides on a little and dims, then the next page shows in its place, so you can tell the swipe took without looking at the arrow. A swipe called off still springs back, and with Reduce Motion the page stays put. #508
  • A password manager extension you choose in Settings › Passwords › Password manager now counts even when Zigzag doesn't know it by name, like Litewarden: Zigzag stops offering to save and fill, shows no key icon in sign-in fields and leaves passkeys to it. An extension that turns the browser's own password saving off, as Bitwarden and 1Password can in Chrome, is listed there and counted the same way, until it turns that back, or you turn it off or remove it. #513
  • Sites you let use your location get it. Before, macOS never asked whether Zigzag could use your location and Zigzag wasn't listed in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services, so a site you allowed, like Google Maps, never found where you are. Now the first site you allow makes macOS ask once. With Location Services off for the whole Mac, or refused for Zigzag, the site is told no at once instead of waiting.
  • ⇧-clicking a link that the site opens by itself, as on YouTube and parts of Google, opens it in Zigzag Mini like any other ⇧-clicked link, instead of as a pop-up tab. Thanks to Lakeland97. #522
  • Extensions that open a minimized window to load a page in the background, like Property Mate, no longer bring a window to the front. As in Chrome, it goes straight to the Dock, and one opened unfocused opens behind your window, which keeps focus. Thanks to Lakeland97. #521
  • Sites that keep your sign-in in a tab's session storage, like Microsoft 365, keep you signed in after Zigzag quits and opens again on your last session, or restarts for an update. A tab that was asleep, or open as Zigzag quit, gets its page's session storage back before the page loads, as in Chrome. It's kept in your profile's folder, readable only by you, never for a private window, and it goes when you clear the site's data or close the tab. With On launch set to Open a new window it's dropped. #502
  • A link a site or extension takes off on purpose so a plain click opens its own viewer, like a quoted tweet in Old Twitter Layout, still opens in a tab on ⌘-click or middle-click, now through one rule in Zigzag that any site or extension can use: keep the address in `data-zigzag-href` (docs/extensions.md). A plain click still opens the viewer. Thanks to Lakeland97 for the design. #517
  • A rule that hands downloads to an app, a script or a service can now match the file's own type, like torrent or pdf, not only its address. A tracker's download address often names no file, so its .torrent downloads can now reach the downloader whatever the address looks like. #524
  • Opened for a Zigzag Mini link, an installed site or AI apps alone, Zigzag no longer drops the last session's sign-ins: when you open a browser window and the session comes back, its session cookies and sessionStorage come back with it, so Microsoft 365 and similar sites keep you signed in. #502
  • A tab's chevron goes away once the last tab nested under it leaves, instead of staying behind and doing nothing, and dragging a tab right to nest it under the tab just above now shows the line at the depth it will land. Thanks to bkonia. #526
  • Holding Back or Forward with the mouse or trackpad, then sliding onto a page in the list and letting go, goes to that page again, as with a finger. Before, letting go there did nothing in the sidebar's top row and the bar above the page. #509
  • An extension pinned to the address, like 1Password, shows its whole icon centered in its button. Before, an icon that arrived after the button was drawn sat a little off and soft. In Site Controls the pin mark sits in the tile's corner instead of over the icon.
  • 1Password's toolbar icon now matches Zigzag's toolbar: on a dark space it shows its dark-toolbar icon even when the Mac is in light mode, and the other way around.
  • Pressing and holding Back or Forward in the sidebar, then sliding down, no longer drags the whole window: the list opens and you can slide onto a page and let go, with the mouse as with a finger.
  • With a link open in Zigzag Mini over its page, ⌘T opens the browser's new tab bar with the Mini out of the way instead of acting behind it, and Esc goes to the sidebar's picked rows, its fields or the command bar when one of them has the keys. Thanks to Lakeland97. #528

0.9.4

November 24, 2026

Reader redesigned to feel like part of Zigzag, Capture like Arc's for an area, an element or the page, Handoff to your iPhone, iPad and other Macs, ⌘Z for split changes, a sidebar width every window keeps, typed and held zoom, Zigzag Mini for shift-clicked links in place of Peek, a choice to start fresh on launch, new glass colors for spaces, Always and Never Translate, a calmer Settings, touch on a touch display, and Your Zigzag Card.

New

  • Zoom keeps going while you hold − or + in Site Controls, and ⇧-clicking the percentage lets you type one, like 130%, instead of stepping to it. A plain click still goes back to Actual Size. #395
  • Reader looks and works like the rest of Zigzag. Wide now fills the window inside its margins instead of stopping at a narrow column, Medium stays a book's line length, and Narrow is a little narrower. Large pictures and code blocks run past the text into the margins when the window has room, and pictures are centered rather than on the text's left edge. The title is lighter, the author is no longer repeated at the top of the text, the site's icon lines up with the text, and the spacing follows one steady rhythm. The Reader button in the address bar shows pressed while Reader is on. Aa sits in the article's top-right corner, fades away as you read down and comes back when you scroll up or move the pointer near the top. It opens Zigzag's own panel right under it, as View › Reader Text and Colors does, with each choice named: Text Size steps as page zoom does (− 100% +, the percentage puts it back), Typeface, Column Width, and Colors as round dots, like picking an accent color in System Settings, the chosen one ringed in the accent color. Auto is split between its light and dark paper and takes the barest hint of the space's color, the text as dark (or light) as before. Changing a choice keeps the line you were reading in place, and the text settles in as Reader opens, a plain fade with Reduce Motion. #455
  • Handoff works as in Safari. The page you're on in the front window shows up on your iPhone, iPad or other Mac to pick up there, and follows you as you switch tabs or go to another page. Private windows, Zigzag Mini, web apps, Zigzag's own pages, error pages, and files or `localhost` pages on your Mac are never offered. With Zigzag as your default browser, a page handed off from Safari or another device opens in a new tab of the front window, in its own space if you chose one for the site. It follows the Handoff setting in System Settings › General › AirDrop & Handoff. #456
  • ⌘Z takes back a change to a split's tiles. Each finished drag of a line between pages is one step, and so is Reset Tile Layout; ⇧⌘Z does it again. It still works after you switch to another tab. Thanks to Lakeland97. #435
  • With the address bar above the page, the pin star sits at the end of the address pill, so the page can be pinned from there. As in the address field, it's left out where there is nothing to pin: Zigzag's own pages, a new tab and private windows. Thanks to Lakeland97. #437
  • Capture works like Arc's. File › Capture… (⇧⌘2), Capture in Site Controls or the command bar dims the page: point at a part of the page and click to take it, even one that runs past the window, or drag over it to take an area, which you can then move and resize by its corners and sides before pressing Return or Capture. Full Page takes the whole page as ⇧⌘2 did before, and Visible takes what's on screen. Esc or Cancel leaves. The picture is copied right away and shows in a small card in the page's corner with Save, Share and a thumbnail you can drag into another app; Save puts it in your download folder, named after the page and the time. The picture is as sharp as your screen, at any zoom and in a split, and nothing is kept anywhere else, in private windows too. #457
  • The sidebar's edge can be dragged while it's out over the page, as it can while it takes room in the window, and the width it leaves is the width every new window starts with, after a restart too; double-clicking the edge puts the usual width back. #431
  • Your Zigzag Card is in Zigzag › About Zigzag: a card with your name and the day you started using Zigzag. Under it you pick its finish: your space's color, Holographic, Graphite or Paper, the chosen one ringed in the accent color, and Zigzag keeps your choice. The card turns toward the pointer with its light following, and a click turns it over. Save as Image… makes a sharp picture with a soft shadow on a clear background, and Share… opens the Mac's share menu. It's made on your Mac, and nothing is sent unless you share it.
  • Reader has a Space color. The article takes the colors of the space you're in, the same color as the sidebar, light or dark as the space is, with text and links kept dark (or light) enough to read easily. It changes right away when you switch spaces or change the space's color. #455
  • Peek is now Zigzag Mini: shift-click a link (or Open Link in Zigzag Mini) and it opens in a Mini over your window, the same as links from other apps; ⌘O moves it into a tab next to where you clicked, Esc closes it. It opens in the page's own space and profile. From a private window it's private too: it says Private at its top and is never saved to History. Open Link in Peek in a link's menu is now Open Link in Zigzag Mini, and the getting-started guide's shift-click step shows Zigzag Mini.
  • On launch can start fresh: Pick Up Where You Left Off is the default, and Start Fresh opens one new window, keeps the previous session for History › Reopen Last Session (with its back and forward history), and doesn’t save the new session until a page actually loads. Thanks to Lakeland97. #433
  • Translating works as in Chrome and Safari. The offer on a page in another language has a … menu with Always Translate (the language), Never Translate (the language) and Never Translate This Site. On a page in another language, a translate button in the address bar opens the same choices, with Translate or Show Original; it stays pressed while the page is translated. Settings › Translation lists the languages you always or never translate and the sites you never translate, each with Remove; with no language to always translate yet, it points to the translate button. A private window never saves a site to the list: Never Translate This Site there lasts until the window closes.
  • New spaces start in Slate, a clear, light glass with the desktop showing through, nearly white with a barely cool tint on a light Mac and deep slate on a dark one. The space editor has seventeen glass colors beside solid white: neutral Slate, Stone and Smoke; Mist, Dew, Shell and Orchid, near-white glass with just a faint tint; cool Azure, Navy, Seafoam, Pine and Fern; warm Citron, Coral and Red; and Iris and Pink. All but Smoke and Navy are light tints of clear glass, so the desktop stays plainly in view instead of under a gray haze; Smoke and Navy keep the window dark with light text, and Citron and Coral keep it light. A few have a finish of their own: Stone a fine linen texture, Smoke and Coral a light grain. Iris, Coral and Navy get a little deeper toward the bottom. Your spaces keep the colors they have now. A space in one of these colors from an earlier 0.9.4 beta moves to its clearer version; for the earlier betas' other colors, Use Default Colors gives the nearest new color.
  • A site that turns Zigzag away can be told it's in another browser. User agent in Site Controls offers Default, Safari, Chrome and Firefox, or Custom… to type one, and the page reloads with it. It covers the site's subdomains, its Zigzag Mini pages, installed apps and pop-ups, and Settings › Privacy › Sites lists it with a × to remove. #392
  • Mute Tab is always in a tab's menu in the sidebar, and in a pinned page's or favorite's while it's open, so a tab can be muted before it plays anything. It stays muted as you go to other pages in it, reload it or it goes to sleep. Thanks to bkonia. #490
  • Settings › General has Install updates automatically, on by default. Turn it off to stay on the version you have. Zigzag still checks, and a new version shows on the update card as available: Update downloads it and restarts into it, and Skip stops offering that version until a newer one comes out. Check for Updates… still finds a skipped version. Thanks to Lakeland97. #492
  • Zigzag works with a touch display and with Sidecar on an iPad. Touch and hold a tab, a folder, a space or the empty sidebar to open its menu. Tapping links that open a new window works like clicking them, and tapping outside a panel or menu closes it. A tap on a row shows its × and other buttons that otherwise only appear under the pointer. The strips that resize the sidebar and split panes, and a row's ×, also answer a finger just beside them. Move away from the menu instead and the tab is picked up, to reorder it, drop it in a folder, onto another tab or the page for a split, or onto a space. Swipe one finger sideways across the sidebar to change spaces, pinch in on a page to see all tabs (pinch out on one to open it), and swipe two fingers sideways on a page to go back or forward.   • On a touch display, while the sidebar is hidden, tap the top corner where the traffic lights live: they show with the sidebar toggle, then fade after a few seconds. Tap the toggle and the sidebar shows in the window, as a click on it does; drag from it and the sidebar follows your finger. In a split view the toggle is a button in the top pane's strip, before Back. Nothing sits over the page otherwise, and the mouse and trackpad work as before.
  • On a touch display, tapping an open favorite shows its ×, and tapping the Tabs label shows Clear, as hovering does with the pointer.

Fixed

  • A solid space with the sidebar on hover no longer stutters on a 120 Hz display. The sidebar that slides out on hover is a card of glass, and in a solid space it was made of Liquid Glass; that glass view stays in the window while the sidebar is tucked away, and Liquid Glass anywhere in a window holds the whole window to 80 frames a second on a ProMotion display, so the page stuttered with the sidebar hidden and was smooth with it shown.
